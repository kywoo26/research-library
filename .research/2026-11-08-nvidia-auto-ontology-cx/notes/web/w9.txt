Agents Don't Fail on Models. They Fail on Context.
Why do AI agents struggle to deliver accurate, traceable answers, and how does a governed context layer help them succeed?
Explore the Dell AI Data Platform
(opens in a new tab)
By
Chhandomay Mandal
|
October 28, 2026
Topics in this article:
Artificial Intelligence
Innovation
Artificial Intelligence Services
Key takeaways:
3 min read
AI agents don't struggle with reasoning. They struggle with scattered context, business definitions and lineage across separate tools, which shows up as latency, token cost, and answers that are hard to trace.
The Dell AI Data Platform turns distributed, siloed enterprise data into one governed context layer through four purpose-built data engines (analytics, processing, search and orchestration) plus PowerScale, ObjectScale and Lightning File System storage engines.
At GTC Berlin, NVIDIA introduced the Agent Knowledge Foundation, a modular set of open models and accelerated libraries for the agent knowledge layer, explicitly not a platform or framework, and Dell plans to support it within the Dell AI Data Platform.
Specific NVIDIA components (Nemotron Retriever models, Auto-Ontology, and CUDA-X libraries like cuVS, cuDF and cuGraph) can be adopted independently to strengthen stages like parsing/embedding/reranking, initial business-meaning mapping, and GPU-accelerated vector indexing.
The architecture stays open and modular by design: customers can bring their own models, ontology and agent framework while Dell makes the underlying data trustworthy.
Ask an agent a deceptively simple question ("How much revenue did we generate last quarter?") and it has to find the right tables, apply your company's definition of revenue, honor exclusions and adjustments, run the calculation, and show where the answer came from. When context, business meaning, and lineage live in separate tools, the agent searches, reasons, and retries. That shows up as latency, token consumption, and infrastructure cost, and as answers that are hard to trace or reuse.
The Context Layer Is the Product
The
Dell AI Data Platform
was built to turn distributed, siloed enterprise data into a single, governed context layer that AI systems can act on. That is Dell IP, and it stands on its own:
Four purpose-built
data engines
(analytics, processing, search and
orchestration
) that discover, transform, govern and retrieve data where it already lives, rather than migrating it into a new silo.
Storage engines
(PowerScale, ObjectScale and Lightning File System) with decoupled compute and storage, so data engines and capacity scale independently.
Open table and file formats, 50+ connectors, and governance, access control and cyber resilience built into the data layer rather than bolted on.
The advancements we walked through at our October 6,
AI Data Platform event
(agent-ready governed access, trusted enterprise context, automated hybrid pipelines and data intelligence that tells you what you actually have) are platform capabilities, not integrations. They are deliberately engine-agnostic and do not depend on any single model or accelerator vendor. That is the point of a modular architecture.
A Complementary Set of Components From NVIDIA
This week at GTC Berlin, NVIDIA introduced the Agent Knowledge Foundation, a modular collection of open models and accelerated libraries for the knowledge layer agents depend on. It is not a platform or an agent framework, and NVIDIA is explicit about that: the components are built to complement the data systems, ontologies, context engines, and agent experiences organizations already run. We are looking forward to supporting it in the Dell AI Data Platform with NVIDIA.
Within our architecture, it gives customers another strong option for specific stages.
NVIDIA Nemotron
Retriever models can strengthen parsing, embedding, and reranking inside our data and context engines. NVIDIA Auto-Ontology can provide a quick starting point for business meaning that experts refine and our governance and lineage capabilities certify.
NVIDIA CUDA
-X libraries (cuVS, cuDF, and cuGraph) enable NVIDIA CPU and GPU acceleration of vector indexing, data preparation and relationship analysis. Each can be adopted independently, which is exactly how customers should expect to consume them.
And, Not Or
Our AI data platform is designed to make enterprise knowledge more useful to AI across systems, teams and business domains, while respecting existing access controls and governance requirements. The result we're after is more accurate, grounded answers and faster retrieval at enterprise scale, with lower reasoning and token cost, while complementing the data platforms, ontologies and agent frameworks customers already run. Our commitment is to openness: bring your models, your ontology, your agent framework. We'll make your data trustworthy underneath it.
Specific capabilities and availability will follow our standard release cadence.
Ready to see how a governed context layer can make your AI agents faster, more accurate and easier to trust? Explore the
Dell AI Data Platform
to learn how its purpose-built data engines, enriched by complementary NVIDIA technologies like the Agent Knowledge Foundation, can turn your distributed enterprise data into grounded, enterprise-scale answers your agents can act on.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
What is the Dell AI Data Platform?
I
t's the Dell architecture for turning distributed, siloed enterprise data into a single, governed context layer that AI systems can act on. It's built from four purpose-built data engines (analytics, processing, search and orchestration) and storage engines (PowerScale, ObjectScale and Lightning File System) with decoupled compute and storage.
What is NVIDIA's Agent Knowledge Foundation, and how does it relate to the Dell AI Data Platform?
Announced at GTC Berlin, it's a modular collection of open models and accelerated libraries for the knowledge layer AI agents depend on, not a platform or agent framework itself. Dell plans to support it within the Dell AI Data Platform as a complementary option, not a replacement for existing platform
capabilities.
What storage considerations matter for agentic AI systems that query enterprise data?
Agentic systems need governed access, trusted context and lineage built into the data layer itself rather than bolted on, so agents can retrieve accurate answers without separately reasoning across siloed tools. Decoupled compute and storage, as in PowerScale, ObjectScale, and Lightning File System, let data engines and capacity scale independently as agent workloads grow.
Can the Dell AI Data Platform support AI agents at scale?
Yes. The platform provides agent-ready governed access, automated hybrid pipelines and data intelligence as core capabilities, built to be engine-agnostic so they don't depend on any single model or accelerator vendor.
Can the Dell AI Data Platform work with our existing storage, ontology and agent framework?
Yes. Dell has stated a commitment to openness: customers can bring their own models, ontology and agent framework, and the platform is designed to make their existing data trustworthy underneath it rather than requiring a rip-and-replace.
