BIRD questions whose gold filters on an aggregate are answered at 41% (33/80 pooled over four runs) against a 76.7% baseline, and the prompt said nothing about them: HAVING appeared twice, both times as an alias scoping rule, and the Aggregation section only covered FILTER and CASE WHEN. The retrieved exemplars decide whether HAVING appears at all -- all 14 questions given a HAVING exemplar emitted one, all 6 without emitted none -- but they carry no accuracy (5/14 vs 2/6), because they transfer query shape and not the threshold, the grouping or the cast.

Add two rules the exemplars cannot convey:

- route a condition on an aggregated value to HAVING rather than WHERE, since a WHERE predicate tests single rows before grouping
- cast to REAL before dividing, including inside HAVING and before scaling a ratio by 100

Both follow BIRD's own conventions: gold casts to REAL in 57 of its 68 divisions, and the dataset paper's canonical Numeric Computation example is CAST(SUM(...) AS REAL) * 100 / COUNT(...).
